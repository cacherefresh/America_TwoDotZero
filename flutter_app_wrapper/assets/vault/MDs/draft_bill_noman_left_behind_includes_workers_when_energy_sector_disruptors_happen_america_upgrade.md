National Family Transition Security Act of 2027
Two-Page Summary
The Problem
Every time America shifts its energy economy — coal, oil, manufacturing — entire towns get destroyed. Families lose homes, kids leave school, and communities turn into Rust Belts. Current programs are too slow, too small, and depend on Congress fighting every year. We can do better.
The Solution
Create a permanent “Family Transition Security Fund” inside the Department of Labor. It works like trade credit insurance — but instead of protecting businesses, it protects American families and towns. When a county loses more than 5% of its jobs in two years because of energy industry changes, help turns on automatically. No waiting. No political battles.
How It’s Paid For

2 cents per barrel on all U.S. oil and natural gas produced domestically
0.1 cent per kilowatt-hour on electricity bills nationwide

These tiny fees are almost invisible to consumers but raise roughly $120–150 million per year — enough to protect communities without new taxes or big government.
What the Money Actually Does

Up to 2 years of wage insurance so families don’t lose their homes
Fast job retraining and skills certifications
Help paying to move for a new job
Grants so local small businesses can grow or start up
Support for towns to attract new employers

Timeline to Save a Town

First 6 months: Immediate wage support and job help
Next 18 months: Training and business grants kick in
By year 3–5: New jobs arrive and the town stabilizes

Key Features

Automatic triggers — no waiting for Congress to act
Runs through the Department of Labor, focused on workers
Money stays in a locked trust fund — cannot be raided
Annual public reports on every dollar spent

Bottom Line
We are the richest nation in history. We don’t have to keep sacrificing towns and families every time technology or the market changes. This fund is simple insurance: the industries that create the risk pay a small fee to protect the people who built them. No more Rust Belts. No more discarded Americans.

### DRAFT BILL EXAMPLE:

National Family Transition Security Act of 2027
SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.
This Act may be cited as the "National Family Transition Security Act of 2027".
SECTION 2. PURPOSE.
To establish an automatic, self-sustaining trust fund that protects American families and communities from economic disruption caused by shifts in the energy sector, providing rapid support for workforce retraining, wage insurance, relocation, and local economic stabilization so that no community becomes a Rust Belt.
SECTION 3. DEFINITIONS.

"Energy Transition Event" means a county or Tribal area experiencing a loss of more than 5% of total employment within any 24-month period due to changes in fossil fuel extraction, refining, or related industries.
"Fund" means the National Family Transition Security Trust Fund established under Section 4.

SECTION 4. ESTABLISHMENT OF THE TRUST FUND.
There is established in the Treasury of the United States a trust fund to be known as the "National Family Transition Security Trust Fund". The Fund shall consist of:
(a) a surcharge of two cents ($0.02) per barrel on all crude oil, natural gas liquids, and condensates produced in the United States;
(b) a fee of one-tenth of one cent ($0.001) per kilowatt-hour on electricity sales nationwide;
(c) any amounts appropriated by Congress; and
(d) investment earnings on amounts in the Fund.
The Secretary of the Treasury shall deposit these revenues into the Fund on a quarterly basis. The Fund shall be managed by the Department of Labor.
SECTION 5. AUTOMATIC BENEFITS AND USES OF FUNDS.
Upon certification of an Energy Transition Event, funds shall be released automatically for:

Workforce retraining and skills certification programs;
Wage insurance for up to 24 months to prevent sharp income loss;
Relocation assistance for affected workers and families;
Grants to local governments and small businesses in affected communities;
Technical assistance to help towns attract new industries.

No annual appropriation from Congress shall be required. Benefits shall begin within 90 days of a trigger being met.
SECTION 6. ADMINISTRATION.
The Secretary of Labor shall administer the Fund through an independent board. Annual reports shall be submitted to Congress detailing disbursements, outcomes, and community stabilization metrics.
SECTION 7. EFFECTIVE DATE.
This Act shall take effect 180 days after the date of enactment.

White Paper: National Family Transition Security Act of 2027
Executive Summary
America’s energy economy is dynamic, but too often the human cost of change is ignored. The National Family Transition Security Act creates a permanent, automatic trust fund — modeled on trade credit insurance — that stabilizes families and communities instead of letting them collapse into Rust Belt conditions. It is funded by a tiny surcharge on domestic energy production, requires no annual congressional appropriations, and triggers support the moment economic pain hits. No town gets left behind.
Problem
Energy transitions have repeatedly hollowed out communities. When industries shift, workers lose jobs, families lose homes, schools lose students, and downtowns die. Existing programs are too slow, too small, and require constant political battles.
Solution
A dedicated trust fund with automatic triggers. Money flows fast — within months — for retraining, wage support, relocation, and local business development. The goal is stabilization: keep people in their homes, kids in school, and communities viable while they adapt.
Funding
A two-cent-per-barrel surcharge on U.S. oil and gas production (roughly $100–120 million per year at current ~13.6 million barrels per day) plus a tiny electricity fee. These costs are negligible at the pump or on the bill but create a reliable revenue stream.
How the Money Flows
Energy producers → small surcharge paid quarterly → locked trust fund at Department of Labor → automatic release when job-loss triggers are met → direct help to families and towns.
Timeline for Stabilizing Towns

Months 0–6: Immediate wage support and job placement.
Months 6–24: Retraining, certifications, and small business grants.
Years 2–5: Economic diversification and new employers arrive.
Outcome: No long-term decline — families stay, communities recover.

Governance
Administered by the Department of Labor, not Energy, to focus on people rather than picking energy winners. Independent board, strict transparency, and annual public reports.
Conclusion
This is not welfare. It is insurance — paid for by the industries that create the risk — to protect the human infrastructure of our nation. When we are the richest country in history, we can afford to make sure no American family or town is discarded during progress.
